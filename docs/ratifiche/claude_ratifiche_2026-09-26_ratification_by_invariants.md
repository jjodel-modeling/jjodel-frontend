## Decision

A human gate controls something only when the cadence of the decisions it gates is below the human's bandwidth. Above that threshold the gate is a latency and a signature, not a control: the decision is taken by whoever wrote the recommendation, the human carries the responsibility for a text he did not read. The protocol had already moved half way (RC-21 adopts recommended answers of Claude Code unattended, RC-23 turns the visual GO into a sampled one from 2026-10-03), but the other half still waited for a preventive «si»: R- rows numbered and parked until ratified, discoveries closing with a list of questions for Alfonso, the Phase 1 hard stop read as a human gate. Four rules move the control from decisions to invariants, and from before to after.

**RC-25, decisions proceed, ratification is asynchronous and revocable.** The project chat answers its own design questions the way RC-21 answers Claude Code's: it takes the recommended option, writes the R- row at once with the marker `provisional, unattended`, and proceeds. Alfonso does not ratify rows one by one; he receives a digest at the close of every lane (in the chat, not as a new artifact: the tracker idea stays set aside), with the decisions ordered by consequence and the three most consequential first, and he may veto any of them. A veto on a decision still on its branch is a revert on that branch before merge; a veto after merge is a lane of its own. Silence does not block: the marker changes to `ratified by digest <date>` when Alfonso acknowledges the digest, or the row is reverted when he vetoes; until then the work goes on. The five-adoption cap of RC-21 is removed: the cap was a proxy for «too much to read», and the digest replaces it. Amends RC-21.

**RC-26, the pre-approval list is closed and short.** Only these decisions wait for Alfonso before the work continues: an edit in the critical zone (Layer Impact Report, as today); a change to an exported interface that breaks a consumer outside the lane; the amendment of an R- row already ratified by Alfonso (not of a provisional one); the deletion of a file or of persisted data; anything that changes what the MODELS demo shows or leaves out; the model, the effort and the cost of the sessions; the push. Everything else proceeds under RC-25. Consequences on the artefacts: the «Questions for Alfonso» section of a discovery report becomes two sections, «Decisions taken (unattended)» with the recommendation adopted for each, and «Decisions awaiting Alfonso», which may contain only items of this list; the Phase 1 hard stop stays a session boundary (the report must exist before Phase 2 is written) but is no longer a human gate: the chat writes the Phase 2 prompt as soon as the report is in the repo. Amends P13 and the prompt template (the Phase 2 hand-off).

**RC-27, verification replaces ratification where a second opinion is needed.** A recommendation whose adoption touches more than one exported interface, or chooses between design options with different data models (the bag, the persistence, a migration), is not adopted on the chat's reading alone: the chat runs an adversarial verification by a second agent, with the report and the proposed decision as input and one line `Verified: <what was checked, what would falsify it>` as output, before writing the provisional row. The verification is minutes, not hours; its line goes in the R- row. Alfonso is not the second opinion: his attention goes to the digest, not to the reading he would not do anyway.

**RC-28, the gate is measured.** Every request that waits for Alfonso records the time from the request to the answer; every checkpoint reports, for the lanes it covers, how many decisions were taken unattended, how many waited, the median wait, and how many vetoes the digests produced. These are the data on which the pre-approval list of RC-26 is shortened or lengthened, and on which the visual GO moves to sampling (RC-23, RC-15). A list that produces no veto in two weeks is a list to shorten; a veto on a decision that did not wait is the signal to lengthen it, with the decision's class named.

## Rationale

Ratification in blocks («as recommended», 13 answers on 2026-09-25, 18 the same day, 11 today) was already the practice; RC-25 stops paying the round trip for it. The quality argument is honest on both sides: some decisions taken unattended will be worse than the ones Alfonso would take reading with attention, and the veto will come late; but that attention is not being spent today, so the quality is already lost, and the digest brings the few decisions that matter back in a form a human can follow. RC-26 keeps the human where he has a comparative advantage (irreversible actions, the demo's scope, cost) and nowhere else. RC-27 puts the second opinion where it is cheap. RC-28 keeps the whole thing falsifiable, in the line of RC-15: nothing is relaxed without data, nothing is tightened without a veto to point at.
